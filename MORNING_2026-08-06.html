It was dead when I found it, twice.

What the red team found (worse than the incident)

ACE-Step is a shared singleton that both apps kill on sight. Both installs point at the same ACE-Step and the same port -- deliberately shared. But every time either app starts a video render, it evicts ACE-Step with no check for whether it is busy. So starting a video in one app silently kills a music generation in the other. This fires during ordinary use, not just restarts, and it predates tonight. Being fixed with a shared GPU lease both installs honor, designed to fail open so a broken lease can never wedge either app.
v1 could still kill V2. I had scoped V2's worker-eviction so it can only touch its own processes. The reviewer pointed out the reverse was still wide open. Fixed defensively in v1 (see decision 5).
Two real Quick Video races. Double-clicking Create could submit two jobs. And dropping your own photo while an image was still generating would let the stale generated image quietly overwrite your upload afterward. Both fixed with a proper guard and a generation token.
What held up under attack: the ratified engine command matches your v16 command flag-for-flag; cancel really kills the whole process tree; no shell-injection path; the GPU-busy retry is properly bounded; port/mutex/socket/shortcut isolation between the two apps is genuinely disjoint.
